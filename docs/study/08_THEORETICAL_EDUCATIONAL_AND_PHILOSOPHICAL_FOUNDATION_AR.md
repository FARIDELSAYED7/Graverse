# GRAVERSE — الأساس الفكري والتربوي والفلسفي (Theoretical, Educational & Philosophical Foundation)

> **الملف (08)**.

---

## 1. Space Heritage

- **Idea:** space hardware as physical traces (artifacts) of human exploration — not just "abandoned junk".
- **Why meaningful:** each object carries purpose, mission, science, fate, location. On Moon/Mars (no wind/erosion), the artifact persists as left — a durable trace.
- **GRAVERSE:** treats hardware as archive entries with provenance, not images only. Digital museum can show 30 with 3 views simultaneously (vs physical museum showing 1-2).

---

## 2. Mission vs Legacy

- **Idea:** end of mission ≠ end of object's historical/scientific value.
- **Example:** Apollo 11 LRRR — mission ended 1969, but device still operates today measuring lunar recession. `apollo-15-lunar-rover` etc.
- **GRAVERSE:** separates mission status (did objectives complete?) from hardware status (what is object doing now?). This is the central intellectual question.

---

## 3. Digital Museums

- **Idea:** preserving relationships, context, provenance > preserving images alone.
- **GRAVERSE:** relation graph (581 edges), source registry (96), claim labels, dataNotes, derived records with original wording. "Connected records" on detail pages. Research Archive with method/coverage/limits.

---

## 4. Storytelling

- **Collection of facts ≠ narrative.** Narrative connects: purpose → design → action → discovery → legacy.
- **GRAVERSE:** Museum narrative sequence (purpose → journey → science → fate); Stories surface (curated reading paths); Academy journeys (steps); Explorer timeline (chronology). Events with descriptions + sources.

---

## 5. Active Discovery

- **Idea:** exploration, comparison, experimentation complement passive reading.
- **GRAVERSE:** Explorer (filters/sort/search/modes), Compare, Simulations (8), Playground (3 games), Rover Builder, Flashcards. All tied to verified records.

## 7. Central intellectual question

**"When a spacecraft stops operating, does its value end?"**

GRAVERSE's answer (from actual content):
- **No.** Mission end ≠ object disappearance. Object may remain (inactive), still operate (LRRR), travel (Voyager), be deliberately impacted (Ranger 7), or be lost contact (Insight).
- Value shifts: from operational science to historical/scientific legacy (what it proved, where it rests, what it enabled).
- The archive preserves: purpose, mission, contributions, fate, location, sources — so the object's story remains accessible and verifiable.

## 8. Intellectual honesty philosophy

- **Unknown > guess.** Missing fields left empty, rendered "Unknown"/"Not recorded".
- **Label derived/simulated/estimated.** Never present model as observation.
- **Preserve original wording.** NASA's literal status beside normalized band.
- **Surface limits.** Research Archive "Honest limitations" (4 explicit), Demo Guide "Do not claim".
- **No fabricated media.** Missing image = deliberate no-image state.
